Purpose and method

How to use this master

Ten documents describe this company in ten different ways. This master reduces them to one controlled narrative, one fact register and one set of channel-ready copy blocks.

Priority 1

Website copy

Six priority pages, production-ready. Metadata, direct-answer blocks, body copy, calls to action and the schema facts that must match the visible page. Section 06.

Priority 2

Brochure copy

Corporate, Shivlakha, GCT, container and wagon brochures built as spreads — not website text dropped into PDF. Section 07.

Phase 3 — held back

Marketing & investor

LinkedIn, campaign and investor presentation content is deliberately not drafted yet. It depends on the fact register and the narrative being frozen first. Section 12.

The order of work

  1. Freeze the controlled fact register (Section 03) and settle the conflicts (Section 04). Nothing downstream is safe until this is signed off.
  2. Approve the master corporate narrative and the boilerplates (Section 05).
  3. Build the website from the copy in Section 06, using only facts that carry a publishable status.
  4. Build the brochures from Section 07, keeping the same figures, dates and status language.
  5. Only then write marketing and investor content — with the investor layer strictly separated from customer-facing claims.

The one rule that matters most. Never convert a target into an achieved result, capacity into production, a Letter of Intent into an operating approval, or Stage-1 clearance into final approval. The status chips used throughout this document exist to make that mistake visible before it reaches print.

Company story

Master corporate narrative

The story is not “a manufacturer becomes a logistics company.” It is an engineering and freight-equipment business extending into rail-linked cargo infrastructure. Keep the transition clear; keep the operating status of each asset separate.

Master narrative · 101 words

Kalyani Cast Tech Limited is an engineering and manufacturing company with capabilities in freight containers, specialised cargo equipment and railway-related products. Its established manufacturing base at Rewari, Haryana, provides the foundation for a broader development at Shivlakha, Kachchh.

At Shivlakha, the company is bringing together container manufacturing, railway wagon manufacturing, a Gati Shakti Cargo Terminal and an ICD under development. The aim is to connect engineering and manufacturing with rail-linked cargo movement and, as the required infrastructure and approvals are completed, an inland EXIM interface.

The customer question stays practical: what is moving, between which locations, in what volume, and how does it move today? Only then can the right equipment, route and handling model be assessed.

One sentence

Kalyani Cast Tech combines engineering, manufacturing and railway-domain experience to develop practical cargo solutions, with Shivlakha extending that base into a rail-linked logistics platform.

Kalyani Cast Tech is an engineering and manufacturing company developing container, wagon and rail-linked cargo capabilities, with an integrated platform taking shape at Shivlakha, Kachchh.

Kalyani Cast Tech Limited builds freight equipment and rail-linked infrastructure around one practical question: how does the customer's cargo need to move?

From manufacturing capability to cargo movement

A container is useful only when it fits the cargo. A rail terminal is useful only when the route works. A wagon matters only when it supports the cargo, loading pattern and operating requirements.

Kalyani is building capabilities across the movement chain: cargo equipment, railway wagons, rail access, terminal handling and an inland EXIM interface under development. We start with the cargo, map the current route and understand the operating constraints before deciding which capability should be evaluated.

Two locations. One cargo-oriented business.

Rewari, Haryana. An established manufacturing base at Mamaria Thethar, with design, development, testing and corner-casting capabilities. Stated container capacity: 6,000–7,000 TEUs per year.

Shivlakha, Kachchh. A rail-linked manufacturing and logistics platform with 10,000 TEUs per year stated container capacity, a Gati Shakti Cargo Terminal, Phase-1 wagon manufacturing and a 30-acre ICD under development.

The customer problem comes first

Customers may be dealing with high inland transport costs, multiple handling points, empty repositioning, equipment availability issues, heavy or high-volume cargo, difficult first/last mile, road dependence or EXIM cargo that needs a controlled inland container and port interface.

Rail is not automatically cheaper. We evaluate the cargo, route, volume, equipment and handling requirements to determine whether a workable commercial case exists.

Engineering supports the commercial solution

Kalyani's manufacturing base includes design, development, fabrication and testing. It supports standard and specialised freight containers and provides the engineering foundation for cargo-specific equipment.

What the terminal does

The terminal connects cargo arriving from the industrial catchment to the railway network. It is where a logistics plan becomes a train movement: cargo is received, handled, assembled into the required train formation and dispatched, subject to railway operating conditions.

How a customer should evaluate rail

Start with the current movement. The Container Should Fit the Cargo — Not the Other Way Around.

Kalyani Cast Tech manufactures standard and specialised freight containers for cargoes where payload, volume, access, loading method, floor design or environmental conditions require more than a standard box.

The right equipment starts with the cargo profile. We look at weight, dimensions, density, loading and discharge method, route restrictions and handling requirements before recommending a configuration.

Standard freight containers

The portfolio includes standard general-purpose and high-cube formats. Detailed dimensions, tare weights, gross ratings and payloads should appear on product pages only after validation against the current approved product master.

Specialised cargo containers

Control

Cross-channel consistency rules

The same fact must read identically on a web page, a brochure spread, a presentation slide and a proposal. Consistency is not a style preference here; it is what makes the figures believable.

One fact, one wording

Numbers, dates, place names and status words come from Section 03 verbatim. No channel gets a "rounder" number or a shorter date.

Status travels with the fact

Capacity is never written as production. A target is never written as a result. A Letter of Intent is never written as approval. A future rakes/day figure never appears without its condition.

Technical depth follows the reader

Website leads with the commercial problem; brochures lead with capability; product data sheets carry the validated specifications. None of them leads with the same opening sentence.

Change one, review all

When a controlled fact changes, the website page, metadata, schema, brochure, presentation and any downloaded PDF are reviewed together. A stale PDF is the most common source of a contradiction.

Calls to action match the channel

Website: start a route conversation. Brochure: request the technical specification or arrange a site visit. Proposal: agree a trial movement. Never the same generic "contact us" everywhere.

Nothing invented, ever

No customers, contracts, volumes, savings, tariffs, utilisation, revenue, order book or approvals that are not in an approved source. When information is missing, the material says so or omits the claim.

Terminology control

Most factual errors in this material will come from a status word, not a number. These are the words that must not be interchanged.

Use this Not this Why it matters

Writing standard

Specific over abstract

Dates, locations, dimensions, capacities, cargo types and routes. Not "innovation", "excellence" or "commitment".

Human rhythm

Vary sentence length. Avoid the mechanical "X enables Y. X supports Y." pattern. Let a short sentence follow a long one.

No filler vocabulary

Avoid world-class, seamless, transformative, robust, unlock, game-changer, cutting-edge, best-in-class and their relatives unless genuinely the right word.

Active voice, plain English

Write as an experienced professional explaining something to another professional. Contractions are acceptable where the audience allows.

Compress

If 100 words carry the meaning, do not use 300. After drafting, remove the sentence that exists only to sound impressive.

Stop when the point is made

No automatic "in conclusion" paragraph. If a closing line is useful, make it specific.
